Research

My research agenda focuses on the political consequences of social stratification. It rests on three pillars: (1) how socio-economic inequalities structure political preferences and participation, and how these dispositions are transmitted from parents to children over the life course; (2) how macro-level political and economic conditions, and their variation across countries, shape these processes over time; (3) how innovative quantitative approaches can shed new light on long-standing problems in longitudinal and life course research.

The motivation is the persistence of social and economic inequality in contemporary democracies, and the question of how structural disadvantage, once established, turns into lasting differences in political orientations. I combine classical sociological theory with longitudinal, multilevel, and statistical learning methods.

The aim is to understand why democratic institutions struggle to remain inclusive under conditions of inequality, and what might be done to widen participation.
