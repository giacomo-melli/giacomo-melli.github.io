How social class is passed down between generations, and what that does to who votes.

I am a sociologist working at the meeting point of social stratification and comparative political sociology. I completed my DPhil at the University of Oxford in September 2026, supervised by Geoffrey Evans and Nan Dirk de Graaf. Before Oxford I studied in Trento and Bologna.

My thesis asks why class inequality in British turnout has proved so durable. Much of the answer lies in the household. Parents who abstain raise children who abstain, and when the major parties converged in the 1990s that withdrawal was handed on rather than reversed. I am now testing the argument across countries, and working on where people believe they stand in the social hierarchy and what follows for their politics.

My work appears in the British Journal of Sociology, Social Science Research, the Journal of European Public Policy, and Research in Social Stratification and Mobility, among others.
